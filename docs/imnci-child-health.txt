IMNCI — Integrated Management of Neonatal and Childhood Illness
Indian NHM and WHO Guidelines for ASHA and Anganwadi Workers

SECTION 1: DANGER SIGNS IN NEWBORNS (0-28 DAYS)

Immediate referral required if any of these signs are present:
- Not feeding well or stopped breastfeeding
- Convulsions or fits
- Fast breathing (60 or more breaths per minute)
- Severe chest indrawing
- Temperature above 37.5°C or below 35.5°C
- Jaundice appearing within 24 hours of birth
- Umbilical redness spreading to surrounding skin
- Bleeding from any site
- Lethargy or unconsciousness
- Bulging fontanelle

ASHA worker action: Do NOT attempt treatment. Arrange immediate referral to PHC or district hospital. Keep baby warm during transport. Continue breastfeeding if possible.

SECTION 2: ACUTE RESPIRATORY INFECTIONS (ARI)

Classification by NHM guidelines:
- No pneumonia (cough/cold): Cough less than 14 days, no fast breathing, no chest indrawing
  Management: Home care. Increase fluids. Continue feeding. Soothe throat with safe remedies.
  Return if: breathing becomes fast, breathing becomes difficult, child becomes sicker, child cannot drink.

- Pneumonia: Fast breathing present (under 2 months: 60+/min, 2-12 months: 50+/min, 1-5 years: 40+/min)
  Management: Give first dose of Amoxicillin. Refer to PHC. Advise mother on danger signs.
  Amoxicillin dose: 2-12 months: 125mg twice daily for 5 days. 1-5 years: 250mg twice daily for 5 days.

- Severe Pneumonia: Chest indrawing, stridor, inability to drink, convulsions, abnormally sleepy
  Management: Give first dose of antibiotic. Refer URGENTLY to hospital. Keep child warm.

SECTION 3: DIARRHEA MANAGEMENT

Classification:
- No dehydration: Less than 2 loose stools, no signs of dehydration
  Management: Plan A — Give extra fluids including ORS. Continue feeding. Zinc supplementation.
  ORS: After each loose stool — under 2 years: 50-100ml. 2-5 years: 100-200ml.
  Zinc: Under 6 months: 10mg daily for 14 days. Over 6 months: 20mg daily for 14 days.

- Some dehydration: Restless/irritable, sunken eyes, drinks eagerly, skin pinch goes back slowly
  Management: Plan B — Give ORS in health facility. 75ml/kg over 4 hours. Reassess after 4 hours.
  If improved: switch to Plan A. If same: repeat Plan B. If worse: switch to Plan C.

- Severe dehydration: Lethargic/unconscious, sunken eyes, unable to drink, skin pinch very slow
  Management: Plan C — Start IV Ringer's Lactate immediately. Refer URGENTLY to hospital.
  If IV not possible: give ORS by nasogastric tube 20ml/kg/hour for 6 hours.

- Dysentery (blood in stool):
  Refer to PHC. Give first dose of Ciprofloxacin. Give ORS. Do NOT give anti-diarrheal medicines.

- Persistent diarrhea (14 days or more):
  Refer to PHC for evaluation. Check for malnutrition. Give Vitamin A if not given in last 6 months.

SECTION 4: FEVER MANAGEMENT

Assessment:
- Check duration of fever
- Check for stiff neck (meningitis sign)
- Check for signs of measles
- Check for ear problems
- Test for malaria in endemic areas (RDT kit)

Malaria management (if RDT positive):
- P. falciparum: ACT (Artesunate-based Combination Therapy) as per national protocol
- P. vivax: Chloroquine for 3 days + Primaquine for 14 days
- Severe malaria: Pre-referral dose of Artesunate. Refer URGENTLY.

Measles:
- Give Vitamin A: Under 6 months: 50,000 IU. 6-11 months: 100,000 IU. 12 months-5 years: 200,000 IU.
- Treat eye/mouth complications. Monitor for pneumonia.
- Severe measles: Refer to hospital.

SECTION 5: IMMUNIZATION SCHEDULE (NHM/UIP)

Birth: BCG, OPV-0, Hepatitis B birth dose
6 weeks: OPV-1, Pentavalent-1, Rotavirus-1, fIPV-1, PCV-1
10 weeks: OPV-2, Pentavalent-2, Rotavirus-2
14 weeks: OPV-3, Pentavalent-3, Rotavirus-3, fIPV-2, PCV-2
9 months: Measles-Rubella-1, JE-1, PCV Booster, Vitamin A (1st dose)
16-24 months: Measles-Rubella-2, JE-2, DPT Booster-1, OPV Booster
5-6 years: DPT Booster-2
10 years: Td (Tetanus-diphtheria)
16 years: Td (Tetanus-diphtheria)

Missed doses: Resume schedule from where interrupted. Do not restart.
Multiple vaccines can be given simultaneously at different sites.

SECTION 6: GROWTH MONITORING

WHO Growth Standards are used in India:
- Weight-for-Age (underweight screening)
- Length/Height-for-Age (stunting screening)  
- Weight-for-Length/Height (wasting screening)
- MUAC (Mid-Upper Arm Circumference) for acute malnutrition

MUAC Classification (6 months to 5 years):
- Green (>=12.5 cm): Normal nutritional status
- Yellow (11.5-12.4 cm): Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM)
- Red (<11.5 cm): Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM)

Weight-for-Age Z-scores:
- Normal: Above -2 SD
- Underweight: Between -2 SD and -3 SD
- Severely underweight: Below -3 SD

Monthly growth monitoring at Anganwadi Centre is mandatory.
Plot on growth chart. Identify growth faltering early.

SECTION 7: BREASTFEEDING COUNSELING

Initiation: Within 1 hour of birth. No pre-lacteal feeds.
Exclusive breastfeeding: Birth to 6 months. No water, no other foods.
Frequency: On demand, at least 8-12 times in 24 hours.
Positioning: Baby's body facing mother, mouth wide open, lower lip turned out.

Common problems and solutions:
- Insufficient milk perception: Reassure. Increase frequency. Check positioning.
- Sore nipples: Check attachment. Allow nipples to air dry.
- Engorgement: Feed frequently. Warm compresses before feed. Cold compresses after.
- Mastitis: Continue breastfeeding. Refer if fever persists or abscess suspected.

Complementary feeding: Start at 6 months with semi-solid foods.
6-8 months: 2-3 meals per day. 9-11 months: 3-4 meals per day. 12-24 months: 3-4 meals + snacks.
Continue breastfeeding until at least 2 years of age.

SECTION 8: WHEN TO REFER

Immediate referral (same day):
- Any danger sign in newborn
- Severe pneumonia or severe dehydration
- Severe malnutrition with complications
- Convulsions or unconsciousness
- Severe malaria
- Suspected meningitis

Urgent referral (within 24-48 hours):
- Pneumonia not responding to first-line antibiotics
- Persistent diarrhea
- Moderate malnutrition with poor appetite
- Fever not responding to treatment after 48 hours

Follow-up visits:
- Pneumonia: Day 3 after antibiotic started
- Diarrhea: Day 3 or when episode ends
- Malnutrition: Weekly for SAM, biweekly for MAM
- Immunization: As per schedule
