Malnutrition Management Guidelines
Indian NHM, ICDS, and WHO Community-Based Management

SECTION 1: CLASSIFICATION OF MALNUTRITION

Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM):
- MUAC less than 11.5 cm (children 6 months to 5 years)
- Weight-for-Height below -3 SD (WHO Growth Standards)
- Bilateral pitting edema (nutritional edema/kwashiorkor)
SAM is a medical emergency. Child is at high risk of death.

Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MAM):
- MUAC 11.5 to 12.4 cm
- Weight-for-Height between -2 SD and -3 SD
- No edema
MAM requires nutritional supplementation and close monitoring.

Stunting (Chronic Malnutrition):
- Height-for-Age below -2 SD: Stunted
- Height-for-Age below -3 SD: Severely stunted
Indicates long-term inadequate nutrition. Cannot be reversed quickly.

Underweight:
- Weight-for-Age below -2 SD: Underweight
- Weight-for-Age below -3 SD: Severely underweight
Reflects both acute and chronic malnutrition.

SECTION 2: SAM MANAGEMENT — FACILITY-BASED (NRC)

Criteria for admission to Nutritional Rehabilitation Centre (NRC):
- SAM with medical complications (any of):
  - Anorexia (fails appetite test)
  - Lower respiratory tract infection
  - High fever
  - Severe dehydration
  - Severe anemia
  - Hypothermia
  - Hypoglycemia
  - Skin lesions
  - Eye signs of Vitamin A deficiency

Phase 1 — Stabilization (Days 1-7):
- Treat infections: Amoxicillin + Gentamicin
- Correct dehydration: ReSoMal (not standard ORS — lower sodium)
- Correct hypoglycemia: 50ml 10% dextrose or sugar water
- Start feeding: F-75 therapeutic milk (75 kcal/100ml)
- Give Vitamin A: As per age-specific doses
- Give Folic acid: 5mg on Day 1, then 1mg daily
- Do NOT give iron in Phase 1

Phase 2 — Rehabilitation (Days 8-42):
- Transition from F-75 to F-100 therapeutic milk (100 kcal/100ml)
- Increase calories to 150-220 kcal/kg/day
- Start iron supplementation: 3mg/kg/day
- Begin catch-up growth monitoring
- Discharge criteria: Appetite returned, infections controlled, weight gain >5g/kg/day for 3 consecutive days

SECTION 3: SAM MANAGEMENT — COMMUNITY-BASED (CMAM)

For SAM without medical complications (passes appetite test, alert, no edema):
- Provide Ready-to-Use Therapeutic Food (RUTF) or locally prepared energy-dense food
- RUTF dose: 175 kcal/kg/day divided into 6-8 feeds
- Weekly follow-up at Anganwadi centre
- MUAC and weight monitoring at each visit
- Recovery criteria: MUAC above 12.5 cm for 2 consecutive visits

Locally prepared therapeutic food (where RUTF not available):
- Recipe 1: Roasted wheat flour + roasted Bengal gram + sugar + oil (4:2:1:1 ratio)
- Recipe 2: Rice/wheat khichdi with added oil, jaggery, and groundnut powder
- Target: At least 175 kcal/kg/day, protein 5g/kg/day

SECTION 4: MAM MANAGEMENT

Community-based management through Anganwadi:
- Supplementary feeding through ICDS (Integrated Child Development Services)
- Take-Home Ration (THR): 500 kcal and 12-15g protein per day for children
- Hot Cooked Meal at Anganwadi centre: For children 3-6 years
- Nutrition and Health Education for mothers

Home-based diet recommendations:
- Include energy-dense foods: oil, ghee, butter in every meal
- Include protein: dal, eggs, milk, groundnuts
- Include micronutrients: green leafy vegetables, fruits
- Feed 5-6 times per day (3 meals + 2-3 snacks)
- Continue breastfeeding if under 2 years

Supplementation:
- Iron and Folic Acid: As per WIFS (Weekly Iron Folic Acid Supplementation)
  Children 6-59 months: 20 drops (20mg iron) + 100mcg folic acid biweekly
- Vitamin A: 9 months and every 6 months until 5 years
- Deworming: Albendazole 400mg every 6 months (after 1 year of age)
- Zinc: 20mg daily during and for 14 days after each diarrhea episode

SECTION 5: VITAMIN AND MINERAL DEFICIENCIES

Vitamin A Deficiency:
- Night blindness, Bitot's spots, corneal xerosis
- Prevention: Vitamin A supplementation every 6 months
- Treatment: 200,000 IU on Day 1, Day 2, and Day 15
- Refer immediately if corneal ulceration

Iron Deficiency Anemia:
- Pallor of palms, nails, conjunctivae, tongue
- Classification: Mild (Hb 10-11), Moderate (Hb 7-10), Severe (Hb below 7)
- Severe anemia: Refer immediately for possible transfusion
- Moderate: Iron syrup 3mg/kg/day for 3 months + folic acid
- Dietary counseling: Include iron-rich foods (jaggery, green leafy vegetables, eggs)

Iodine Deficiency:
- Goiter (enlarged thyroid), developmental delays
- Prevention: Use iodized salt exclusively
- Refer for assessment if goiter detected

SECTION 6: INFANT AND YOUNG CHILD FEEDING (IYCF)

WHO/NHM Recommendations:
- 0-6 months: Exclusive breastfeeding. No water, no other foods.
- 6-8 months: Continue breastfeeding + 2-3 meals of semi-solid food
  Start with mashed rice/roti with dal, mashed vegetables, mashed fruits
- 9-11 months: Continue breastfeeding + 3-4 meals of semi-solid/soft food
  Include egg, fish, chicken (if non-vegetarian), paneer, curd
- 12-24 months: Continue breastfeeding + 3-4 meals of family food
  Add oil/ghee to every meal for energy density

Responsive feeding:
- Feed child directly, assist older children
- Feed slowly and patiently
- Do not force feed
- If child refuses, try different food combinations
- Minimize distractions during feeding

SECTION 7: GROWTH MONITORING AND PROMOTION

Monthly activities at Anganwadi:
- Weigh all children under 5 years
- Plot weight on growth card (Mother-Child Protection Card)
- Identify growth faltering (flattening or declining growth curve)
- Measure MUAC for children 6 months to 5 years
- Counsel mothers on feeding and care practices

Red flags for immediate action:
- Weight loss for 2 consecutive months
- MUAC in red zone (below 11.5 cm)
- Bilateral pitting edema
- Visible severe wasting
- Child not gaining weight despite supplementary feeding

Follow-up schedule:
- Normal children: Monthly at Anganwadi
- MAM children: Biweekly at Anganwadi + home visits
- SAM children (community-managed): Weekly at Anganwadi + weekly home visits
- SAM with complications: Admit to NRC. Follow up after discharge weekly for 2 months.

SECTION 8: ANGANWADI WORKER RESPONSIBILITIES

Daily activities:
- Provide supplementary nutrition to children and pregnant/lactating women
- Conduct pre-school education for 3-6 year olds
- Record attendance and nutrition status

Monthly activities:
- Growth monitoring of all registered children
- Identify malnourished children and refer as needed
- Conduct nutrition and health education sessions
- Coordinate with ASHA worker for home visits

Referral criteria:
- SAM with complications → NRC/District Hospital immediately
- SAM without complications → PHC for assessment, then CMAM
- MAM not improving after 2 months → PHC for assessment
- Any sick child with danger signs → PHC/Hospital immediately

SECTION 9: POSHAN ABHIYAAN (NATIONAL NUTRITION MISSION)

Key targets:
- Reduce stunting by 2% per year (from 38.4% to target of 25%)
- Reduce anemia among young children by 3% per year
- Reduce low birth weight by 2% per year

Community-based events:
- Monthly VHSND (Village Health Sanitation and Nutrition Day)
- Annual Poshan Maah (Nutrition Month) — September
- Annaprashan Diwas (Complementary Feeding Day)

Technology tools:
- ICDS-CAS (Common Application Software) for real-time tracking
- Poshan Tracker app for Anganwadi workers
- Growth monitoring using digital weighing scales
