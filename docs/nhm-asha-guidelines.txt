NHM ASHA Worker Skin Disease Guidelines

SECTION 1: COMMON SKIN CONDITIONS

Ringworm (Tinea corporis)
Ringworm is a fungal infection causing circular, scaly, itchy patches on the skin.
ASHA workers may recommend Clotrimazole 1% cream applied twice daily for 2-4 weeks.
Refer to PHC if no improvement after 4 weeks or if condition spreads.

Tinea Versicolor (Pityriasis versicolor)
A fungal infection causing light or dark patches on skin, commonly on chest and back.
ASHA workers may recommend Ketoconazole 2% shampoo applied topically, left for 10 minutes.
Refer if extensive or recurring.

Scabies
Scabies is caused by Sarcoptes scabiei mite infestation causing intense itching, especially at night.
ASHA workers may recommend Permethrin 5% cream applied from neck to toes overnight.
All household contacts must be treated simultaneously.
Refer to PHC if secondary bacterial infection is suspected.

Contact Dermatitis
Skin inflammation from contact with irritant or allergen. Red, itchy, sometimes blistered skin.
Mild cases: ASHA workers may recommend Calamine lotion for relief.
Identify and remove the causative agent.
Refer to PHC if severe, widespread, or not improving in 7 days.

Heat Rash (Miliaria)
Blocked sweat glands causing small red bumps or blisters in hot conditions.
Talc-free powder and keeping area dry. Avoid heat exposure.
Usually resolves without treatment. Refer if infected.

Mild Eczema (Atopic Dermatitis)
Chronic inflammatory skin condition with dry, itchy, inflamed skin.
Mild cases: Moisturizing cream (non-prescription) applied regularly.
Refer to PHC for moderate to severe cases or if steroids are needed.

Dry Skin (Xerosis)
Dry, rough, flaky or cracked skin often due to weather, bathing habits, or low humidity.
ASHA workers may recommend fragrance-free moisturizing cream or coconut oil applied twice daily.
Advise patient to avoid harsh soaps, use lukewarm water for bathing, and drink adequate water.
Refer if cracking, bleeding, or signs of infection develop.

Acne (Pimples)
Common skin condition with blackheads, whiteheads, pimples on face, chest, or back.
ASHA workers may advise: wash face twice daily with mild cleanser, do not squeeze pimples.
Avoid oily cosmetics. Keep hair clean and off face.
Refer to PHC if severe cystic acne, scarring, or not improving.

Urticaria (Hives / Allergic Rash)
Raised, itchy, red welts on skin that may appear and disappear within hours.
Often triggered by food, medicine, insect bites, or contact with allergen.
ASHA workers should monitor for breathing difficulty or lip/tongue swelling.
Refer IMMEDIATELY if signs of anaphylaxis (difficulty breathing, swelling of face/throat).

Boils (Furuncles)
Painful, red, pus-filled bump caused by bacterial infection of hair follicle.
Apply warm compress several times daily to encourage drainage.
Keep area clean. Do NOT squeeze or attempt to drain.
Refer if multiple boils, fever, or on face.

Minor Wounds and Cuts
Clean wound with clean water. Apply antiseptic (povidone-iodine).
Cover with clean bandage. Check tetanus vaccination status.
Refer if deep wound, bleeding won't stop, or signs of infection (redness, pus, fever).

Minor Burns
Cool under running water for 10-20 minutes immediately.
Do NOT apply ice, butter, toothpaste, or any home remedy.
Cover with clean cloth. Give paracetamol for pain.
Refer if blisters, face/hands/genitals involved, or large area affected.

Dandruff (Seborrheic Dermatitis)
White or yellow flakes on scalp with mild itching.
Use anti-dandruff shampoo (ketoconazole or zinc pyrithione) 2-3 times per week.
Gently massage scalp. Avoid scratching.
Refer if severe, spreading to face, or not improving after 4 weeks.

Fungal Skin Infection (General)
Itchy red or discolored patches, often in warm moist areas (groin, between toes, body folds).
ASHA workers may recommend topical antifungal cream (Clotrimazole or Miconazole) twice daily.
Keep area clean and dry. Wear loose breathable clothing.
Refer if extensive, on scalp, or not improving after 4 weeks.

SECTION 2: CONDITIONS REQUIRING IMMEDIATE REFERRAL

Leprosy (Hansen's disease)
Pale or reddish patches with loss of sensation. Thickened nerves.
DO NOT attempt treatment. Refer immediately to PHC or district hospital.
This is a notifiable disease under NHM guidelines.

Suspected Skin Cancer or Melanoma
Irregular moles, non-healing ulcers, rapidly growing lesions.
DO NOT attempt treatment. Refer immediately to district hospital.

Cellulitis
Spreading red, warm, swollen skin with fever indicates bacterial infection.
Refer immediately. Patient may need IV antibiotics.

SECTION 3: REFERRAL CRITERIA

Refer to PHC when:
- Condition does not improve after 2 weeks of recommended treatment
- Condition involves face, genitals, or large body surface area
- Patient has fever alongside skin condition
- ASHA worker is uncertain about diagnosis
- Child under 5 years with skin infection
- Pregnant woman with any skin condition

Refer immediately to district hospital when:
- Suspected leprosy
- Suspected skin cancer
- Cellulitis with fever
- Extensive blistering or skin peeling
- Signs of anaphylaxis or severe allergic reaction

SECTION 4: DOCUMENTATION

ASHA workers must document:
- Date of screening
- Patient age and sex (no names required for privacy)
- Condition identified
- Treatment recommended or referral made
- Follow-up scheduled
