NHM ASHA WORKER SKIN CONDITIONS HANDBOOK
Source: National Health Mission, Government of India

SECTION 1: SCOPE OF ASHA SKIN SCREENING
ASHA workers are trained to identify and provide first-level care for common skin conditions in rural communities. This handbook defines the conditions within scope, the OTC remedies permitted, and the criteria for referral to a Primary Health Centre (PHC).

SECTION 2: CONDITIONS WITHIN ASHA SCOPE

2.1 RINGWORM (TINEA CORPORIS / TINEA CAPITIS)
Ringworm is a fungal infection caused by dermatophytes. It presents as a circular or ring-shaped red rash with a raised, scaly border and central clearing. It is highly contagious and spreads through direct contact. ASHA workers may recommend Clotrimazole 1% cream applied twice daily for 2-4 weeks. Tinea capitis (scalp ringworm) affecting children requires referral to PHC.

2.2 TINEA VERSICOLOR (PITYRIASIS VERSICOLOR)
Tinea versicolor is a fungal infection caused by Malassezia yeast. It presents as hypo- or hyperpigmented patches on the trunk, neck, and upper arms. More common in hot, humid climates. ASHA workers may recommend Ketoconazole 2% shampoo applied topically for 5-10 minutes daily for 2 weeks.

2.3 SCABIES
Scabies is caused by the Sarcoptes scabiei mite. It presents with intense nocturnal itching, thin irregular burrow tracks, and papules between fingers, wrists, and skin folds. It is highly contagious within households. ASHA workers may recommend Permethrin 5% cream applied to the entire body from neck down overnight. All household contacts must be treated simultaneously.

2.4 CONTACT DERMATITIS (MILD)
Mild contact dermatitis presents as localized redness, itching, and skin irritation at the site of contact with an irritant or allergen. Common triggers include soaps, detergents, plants. If limited to a small area with no blistering, ASHA workers may recommend Calamine lotion for symptom relief.

2.5 HEAT RASH (MILIARIA / PRICKLY HEAT)
Heat rash occurs when sweat ducts are blocked. Presents as small red bumps or blisters on areas covered by clothing. Common in infants and adults in hot humid environments. ASHA workers may recommend keeping the area clean and dry, talc-free powder, and loose cotton clothing.

2.6 MILD ECZEMA (ATOPIC DERMATITIS - MILD)
Mild eczema presents as dry, itchy, inflamed skin patches. In mild cases without secondary infection, ASHA workers may recommend fragrance-free moisturizing cream applied twice daily.

SECTION 3: REFERRAL CRITERIA
ASHA workers must refer to PHC if:
- Condition affects more than 10% of body surface area
- Child under 2 years with skin condition
- Pregnant women with skin condition
- Signs of secondary bacterial infection (pus, fever, warm swelling)
- Condition does not improve after 2 weeks of OTC treatment
- Any suspicion of leprosy (see Section 4)
- Ulcerating lesions, rapidly spreading rash
- Patient reports hair loss with scalp involvement

SECTION 4: LEPROSY SCREENING
Leprosy is caused by Mycobacterium leprae. Early signs include hypopigmented or reddish patches with loss of sensation, thickened peripheral nerves, and painless ulcers on extremities. ASHA workers must NEVER attempt to treat leprosy. Any suspected leprosy case must be referred to PHC immediately for NLEP programme evaluation. Do not advise OTC treatment.

SECTION 5: CONDITIONS OUTSIDE ASHA SCOPE
ASHA workers must refer all of the following to PHC:
- Psoriasis
- Skin ulcers
- Suspected skin cancer or melanoma
- Severe allergic reactions
- Drug reactions
- Viral warts (multiple or rapidly spreading)
- Any condition with systemic symptoms (fever, weight loss, lymph node swelling)
